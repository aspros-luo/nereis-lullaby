# Worldview Foundations (with Status Labels)

> This document distinguishes confirmed direction from open proposals. Only explicitly confirmed material is binding canon.

## Confirmed direction
- The world contains outer gods, remnants of ancient divine beings, systems of faith, and an influential church.
- Mite is an agent of an outer god without knowing it. She should live like an innocent lamb; her kindness, attachments, and everyday emotions are genuine.
- The village is not mere scenery. Villagers' routines, relationships, and instinct to protect Mite can form part of the causal narrative.
- The story is primarily told from Mite's perspective. The player may notice anomalies before Mite does, but “what the player knows,” “what Mite believes,” and “what is actually true” must remain distinct.
- Reference points include Saya no Uta's subjective perception and innocence/horror, Bloodborne's staged world transformation, and Soulslike environmental/item storytelling. These are craft references, not plots to copy.

## World-history material (requires review before canonization)
- The kingdom once conquered neighbors through faith power and later became a theocracy.
- The church increasingly sought power, studied sleeping ancient gods on the world map, and may have used their remnants to disguise or amplify faith power.
- The church may treat remote villages and their residents as part of experiments, rituals, or political plans.
These are existing worldbuilding materials; their details, causality, and timeline require deliberate reconciliation rather than invention by default.

## Mite and the village (core proposal)
- Mite's ordinary routine may objectively form part of a ritual: gathering flowers, visiting neighbors, delivering objects, exploring, or repeating habits.
- She need not knowingly act as a cult leader. She can sincerely help others while unknowingly advancing a larger pattern.
- Villagers may participate gradually while understanding their actions as tradition, routine, or protection of the village.
- The ritual center is undecided: it may be inside the village, in the nearby forest, or distributed between both. A hidden forest base is the current recommendation, not canon.
- A key event may transform the meaning of familiar places, dialogue, and habits, in the way Bloodborne's post-Rom Blood Moon changes the player's understanding.

## Candidate idea: villagers protect Mite
Villagers may collectively protect Mite, potentially forming a force reminiscent of one hundred cultists. Their emotional motive could be protection rather than hatred. This is only a candidate; it is not guaranteed to happen, assigned to an ending, or defined as the final battle.

## Prohibited automatic inferences
- Mite's ignorance does not make her emotions false.
- Villagers participating in a ritual does not mean every villager knows they are performing cult activity.
- Intimacy between Hero and Mite does not automatically decide either character's fate or a major ending.
- One ending example must not be used to infer another game's inevitable future.
