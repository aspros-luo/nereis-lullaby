# Village Character and Secret Matrix (Design Proposal)

> Status: The peaceful surface of the village, a very small number of people knowing Mite is important, the ritual's ultimate goal of bringing the Outer God into the world, and villagers protecting what they believe in after Mite and the protagonist break apart in “Blazing Divine Retribution” are confirmed directions. The six role slots, identities, schedules, clues, and specific reactions below are proposals, not finalized canon.

## 1. Goals and boundaries

The six slots describe narrative functions first; the village is not required to have exactly six important characters.

- Make the village feel like a community even without a main-story event: people work, care for family, form friendships, recover from displacement, and keep private secrets.
- Let players infer meaning from everyday behavior and conflicting evidence. Mite lives within her own understanding and does not proactively analyze the Outer God or ritual.
- Distinguish four information layers: objective fact, what a character knows, what a character believes (possibly incorrectly), and what the player currently infers.
- A hidden identity does not replace personality. Anyone involved in a ritual still needs personal relationships, pressures, and reasons.
- Events may override schedules, but each override needs a cause and end condition. No character may occupy two places in one slot or teleport without cause.
- If a player misses a clue in person, provide an alternate trail through later dialogue, an object, a record, or a changed location.

## 2. Confirmed directions and open questions

### Confirmed
- The village is broadly peaceful. Local residents live alongside people displaced by war.
- Mite does not know she is an agent of the Outer God. Her kindness, attachments, and everyday feelings are genuine.
- A very small number of villagers know Mite is important, but do not know why or understand her true nature. Their care may be sincere.
- The cult ritual ultimately aims to bring the Outer God into the world.
- In “Blazing Divine Retribution,” after villagers learn Mite's identity and Mite breaks with the protagonist, they resolutely protect what they believe in.

### Not yet decided
- Who knows Mite is “important,” how many people know, why they know, and how they learned it.
- Who knows the ritual's ultimate goal, and who only performs ordinary-looking traditions or community activities.
- Who knows Mite is an agent of the Outer God. Do not assume anyone knows this before the world changes.
- The ritual's stages, location, mechanics, participant roles, and objective causal relationship with village life.
- Each character's choices in other endings. The stance in “Blazing Divine Retribution” must not be generalized to other routes.

## 3. Six character-function slots (all proposed)

### A. Caregiver: give Mite a believable everyday home
- **Public role:** An older local woman who may be a neighbor, host, or long-time caregiver; the exact relationship is open.
- **Core motivation:** Wants Mite to eat, rest, and avoid needless trouble. Her care comes from shared life, not a secret assignment.
- **Genuine feelings:** Affection, protectiveness, and habit. Later revelations must not turn all of this into an act.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Presume she does not know the Outer God or Mite's role. Whether she knows Mite is “important” remains open.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning food and household chores; daytime shopping and helping neighbors; evening chores and checking whether Mite has returned.
- **Observable behavior:** Repeated reminders to eat, avoid a road, or come home on a certain day should first read plausibly as care.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** She may discourage Mite from visiting a place without being able to explain why, or insist that “it has always been this way.”
- **Possible ending stance:** If she protects the faith, show private pain and conflict. Whether she ultimately opposes Mite remains open.
- **Risk:** Do not make her a priest or manipulator merely because she is older and caring.

### B. Gentle worship leader: connect public faith with limited hidden knowledge
- **Public role:** A gentle person who leads prayers, festivals, or memorials; no formal title is assumed.
- **Core motivation:** Preserve community stability and shared rituals, helping residents process fear, death, and loss.
- **Genuine feelings:** May sincerely admire Mite's kindness or feel responsible for her for reasons not yet understood.
- **Knowledge boundary:** A candidate for one of the few who know Mite is important, but not automatically aware of her true nature or the whole ritual.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning preparation of the worship space; daytime community requests and newcomer support; evening prayers or private conversations.
- **Possible clues:** Unusual care around an ordinary object or date, an outsized reaction to Mite missing an event, or differences between public and private wording.
- **Ordinary explanation:** A worship leader naturally cares about tradition, timing, and public order. One anomaly does not prove cult involvement.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** Works harder to preserve ritual order or starts avoiding certain questions.
- **Possible ending stance:** May see protecting the faith as a duty to the dead, community, or a personal vow rather than a pursuit of power.
- **Risk:** Priesthood, formal title, and knowledge of the ritual's goal remain open.

### C. Doctor / herbalist: leave everyday evidence that can be reinterpreted
- **Public role:** A doctor, herbalist, or caregiver treating locals and displaced people.
- **Core motivation:** Reduce suffering, earn a living, and choose whom to help first when resources are scarce.
- **Genuine feelings:** Treats Mite as a familiar young neighbor. Medical records do not make this character conspiratorial.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows symptoms and histories, not their supernatural explanation.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning supply and appointment checks; daytime consultations and gathering ingredients; evening case notes and patient visits.
- **Possible clues:** Different residents report similar insomnia, dreams, or brief memory gaps around similar times, while records use ordinary medical explanations.
- **Ordinary explanation:** War trauma, malnutrition, infection, stress, and poor sleep can cause overlapping symptoms.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** The pattern between symptoms, dates, and places becomes harder to ignore.
- **Possible ending stance:** Their priority may remain protecting patients; they need not automatically choose faith or protagonist.
- **Risk:** Case notes are traceable evidence, not an exposition dump.

### D. War-displaced outsider: provide an outside view without making them a suspect
- **Public role:** Someone displaced by war, temporarily living in the village: perhaps a craftsperson, porter, former soldier, or family member. Occupation is open.
- **Core motivation:** Find safety, rebuild a life, search for missing relatives, or save enough to move on.
- **Genuine feelings:** May be grateful for Mite's kindness without treating her as an investigation target.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Unfamiliar with local customs and likely to notice differences, but outsider status does not make them closer to the truth.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning job search or supply queue; daytime work, repairs, or seeking news; evening meals with companions or packing belongings.
- **Possible clue:** They ask about a custom and receive explanations that conflict with another resident's account.
- **Ordinary explanation:** Cultural differences, memory, and regional etiquette can explain disagreement.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** They compare changes with remembered customs or leave to protect family.
- **Possible ending stance:** May protect neighbors or flee with family; not automatically an informant or traitor.
- **Risk:** Do not use “the outsider sees the secret” as a universal detective device.

### E. Same-age friend: ensure friendship is not a disguise for conspiracy
- **Public role:** A local friend around Mite's age, with independent tastes, flaws, and goals.
- **Core motivation:** Enjoy friendship and pursue a personal ambition, such as learning a trade, traveling, or resolving a family problem.
- **Genuine feelings:** Real and reciprocal, allowing jealousy, disagreement, reconciliation, and misunderstanding.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Presume they do not know Mite's importance or the ritual's goal. Closeness to Mite does not make them an insider.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning family work or study; daytime meeting Mite, errands, hobbies, or other friends; evening family duties and occasional plan changes.
- **Possible clue:** Repeats a customary phrase or remembers a small event Mite forgot; this may simply be ordinary memory.
- **Ordinary explanation:** Friends have different memories, private jokes, and shifting emotions.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** Fear, anger, or protectiveness changes their schedule, but they do not suddenly become all-knowing.
- **Possible ending stance:** If they defend the faith, establish emotional conflict first. They might instead refuse to hurt Mite, leave, or seek a third option.
- **Risk:** Do not reinterpret every intimate bond as manipulation for a twist.

### F. Community organizer: connect village cooperation with collective action
- **Public role:** Coordinates storage, supplies, communal work, festival preparation, or village communication; religious status is not assumed.
- **Core motivation:** Keep the village functioning despite scarce resources and new arrivals, while preventing conflict.
- **Genuine feelings:** May see Mite as a reliable helper, a neighbor's child, or a community member.
- **Knowledge boundary:** May arrange repeated activities without knowing whether they objectively relate to the ritual. Whether they have special knowledge is open.
- **Draft schedule:** Morning supply checks and assignments; daytime repairs and distribution; evening results and planning.
- **Possible clues:** The same supply appears in different activities, staffing follows fixed patterns on certain dates, or ledgers differ slightly from spoken explanations.
- **Ordinary explanation:** Reusing tools, bulk purchasing, and fixed shifts are normal community management.
- **Possible post-anomaly change:** Becomes insistent on completing a routine or anxious when a supply is missing.
- **Possible ending stance:** Could protect community order, honor a promise, or fear that stopping the ritual will bring greater disaster.
- **Risk:** Managing supplies does not automatically make this person the ritual's leader.

## 4. Knowledge and belief matrix (initial defaults; all proposed)

| Character slot | Knows Mite is “important” | Knows ritual's ultimate goal | Knows Mite is an Outer God agent | May take part in related activity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Caregiver | Open; presume no | Presume no | Unknown; do not presume | May unknowingly take part through ordinary life |
| B. Worship leader | Candidate: possibly | Open; not automatic | Presume no | May lead public traditions |
| C. Doctor / herbalist | Presume no | Presume no | Presume no | May document related symptoms |
| D. Displaced outsider | Presume no | Presume no | Presume no | Unfamiliar with local customs |
| E. Same-age friend | Presume no | Presume no | Presume no | May attend ordinary community activities |
| F. Community organizer | Open; presume no | Presume no | Presume no | May arrange repeated public activities |

**Interpretation rules**
- Taking part in an activity does not mean knowing it is a ritual.
- Knowing Mite is important does not mean knowing why; knowing the ritual's goal does not mean knowing Mite's identity.
- No slot should know the whole truth until evidence and story rules establish why.
- “Presume no” is a starting point, not a final verdict.

## 5. Schedule design rules

Initial data for each key NPC should include a stable character ID, time slot, one primary location, everyday activity, motivation, event override and reason, actual knowledge, personal beliefs, witnessed-event memories, and player-visible clues.

1. Make the schedule plausible before adding clues.
2. Each override needs a start condition, duration, and recovery condition.
3. A character has only one primary location per slot; special events need explicit transitions.
4. Do not store meta-instructions such as “the player should suspect this person.”
5. Each clue needs an ordinary explanation. Later evidence may weaken it, but twists cannot arbitrarily rewrite the past.

## 6. Reconstructable evidence-chain template (illustrative, not canon)

1. Everyday observation: Mite helps with an ordinary task.
2. Small inconsistency: two residents describe the same custom differently.
3. Repetition: an ordinary object or fixed date appears again.
4. Independent reference: an outsider asks a question but does not supply the answer.
5. Reinterpretation after an anomaly: a changed location, schedule, or object gives earlier details new meaning.
6. Alternate path: a later record, location, or another character's memory preserves the contradiction if a conversation was missed.

The notebook records events, statements, and contradictions the player actually encountered. It does not automatically label theories as objective fact.

## 7. Character stances in “Blazing Divine Retribution”

- The confirmed direction applies to this ending: after learning Mite's identity and after her break with the protagonist, villagers firmly protect what they believe in.
- This does not require one shared motive or make every character a combatant.
- Candidate motives include honoring the dead and tradition, fearing catastrophe, believing the ritual protects the village, preserving community bonds, keeping a vow, or turning to faith after losing hope. None is finalized.
- Characters may grieve, hesitate, try to persuade Mite, or refuse to harm her under certain conditions. Their behavior must follow established relationships and beliefs.
- Do not reduce protecting faith to an evil label or present it as a choice without a cost.
- Design villagers' stances in other endings separately.

## 8. Next step and review questions

Choose three slots for the vertical slice, ideally: one ordinary person who provides genuine emotional safety; one candidate with limited hidden knowledge but not omniscience; and one person who provides traceable evidence through records or an outside perspective. This selection remains a proposal.

Then draft seven days of schedules for those three characters, with three slots per day. Check:
- Do they live plausible lives when the player does nothing?
- Does each anomaly have a credible ordinary explanation?
- Does each character know only what they have reason to know?
- Can the player find an alternate clue after missing one event?
- Does the world change produce at least three observable differences traceable to earlier details?
- Does the conflict in “Blazing Divine Retribution” arise from genuine beliefs and broken relationships rather than everyone suddenly changing sides?
