# Core Gameplay Baseline (First Prototype; Some Details Remain Proposed)

## Target experience

The player accompanies Mite through everyday life and freely explores the village and forest, observing villagers acting on their own. Gradually, the player notices contradictions in ordinary details. Mite continues to live in ways that make sense to her; horror emerges from the player's reinterpretation of environments, event order, and character behavior—not from giving Mite an insight power she actively uses.

## Confirmed gameplay pillars

1. **Everyday activities**: visiting, helping, gathering, resting, joining communal events, and spending time with characters. The exact activity list awaits prototype validation.
2. **Region-based exploration**: enter specific locations through a region map and observe changes tied to time periods, character states, and world phases.
3. **Clue journal**: record encountered events, statements, objects, and contradictions. It is a player-facing evidence organizer, not proof that Mite understands the truth.
4. **Autonomous key NPCs**: important villagers have schedules, relationships, and states; simulation and authored events jointly drive behavior.
5. **Dual meaning of ordinary actions**: some ordinary actions may objectively participate in a ritual without Mite understanding their meaning. The ritual's rules are not finalized.
6. **Staged world transformation**: key narrative events change locations, dialogue, character behavior, and the meaning of objects.

## Daily loop (confirmed framework)

- **Morning**: begin the day and choose an activity or destination.
- **Daytime**: visit, help, explore, gather, or join activities.
- **Evening**: continue activities, revisit characters, and resolve daily events and state changes.

The number of action points, whether actions consume a full period, whether periods can be skipped, and how night events work remain open for prototype validation. Avoid turning daily life into a mandatory checklist.

## Narrative information layers

### What Mite knows
Her inner monologue must be based on actual experience, reasonable memory, and explanations she can understand. Do not have her deliberately analyze the Outer God, ritual mechanics, or her role as an agent unless a credible change in understanding is established later and explicitly approved.

### What the player may infer
The player can notice anomalies through recurring objects, conflicting statements, NPC behavior, location changes, and event order. The clue journal records evidence and contradictions; it must not automatically label speculation as fact.

### What actually happens in the world
World truth must follow consistent causal rules. It may remain undisclosed temporarily, but later twists must be traceable to earlier clues rather than arbitrary retroactive changes.

## Key NPC simulation

The initial model for each key NPC should include:
- current location and time-period schedule;
- relationship state with Mite and other characters;
- current goal or near-term motivation;
- memory of key events;
- behavioral tendencies under pressure.

Start with three villagers whose behavior differs meaningfully. Do not simulate every villager. Authored events may override or adjust schedules, but override rules must be explicit so NPCs do not occupy multiple locations or teleport without cause.

## First vertical slice (proposal)

- One small explorable village;
- Three key villagers with distinct schedules and motivations;
- One forest area;
- Several everyday activities and location interactions;
- A player-facing clue journal;
- One major world transformation;
- At least three observable changes to existing content after the transformation.

## Validation questions

1. Does the player choose activities rather than only advance dialogue?
2. Can the player infer anomalies while understanding why Mite thinks life is normal?
3. Do NPCs feel like people with their own lives, rather than existing only when triggered?
4. After a world transformation, does the player want to revisit familiar places and reinterpret details?
5. Is there a reasonable alternative path to important information if an activity is missed?
6. Can suspense and player curiosity be sustained without an insight meter or Mite knowing about the Outer God?
