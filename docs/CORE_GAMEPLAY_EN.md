# Core Gameplay Proposal (Not Final)

## Target experience
The player accompanies Mite through everyday life, explores the village and forest, observes autonomous villagers, and connects contradictory clues. Key events gradually reinterpret familiar routines and transform the world in stages.

## Recommended gameplay pillars
1. **Daily activities**: visiting, delivering items, gathering, resting, joining communal events, and spending time with characters.
2. **Free exploration**: choose locations and times to find objects, rumors, hidden paths, and environmental changes.
3. **Clue and memory journal**: record events, statements, and contradictions; form hypotheses that unlock new investigative options.
4. **Autonomous character simulation**: villagers act according to schedules, relationships, and event conditions, including when the player is absent.
5. **Unconscious ritual**: Mite's routines and villagers' behavior may objectively form a ritual without the characters understanding its meaning.
6. **Staged world transformation**: key events change familiar locations, dialogue, behavior, and item descriptions.

## Candidate daily loop
- Morning: observe the village state and choose a focus for the day.
- Daytime: spend a limited number of actions on visits, exploration, or everyday tasks.
- Evening: resolve relationships, requests, and autonomous character actions.
- Night: dreams, memories, anomalies, or clue updates.
- Next day: update schedules and world state according to event rules.

This loop is a proposal. Avoid excessive schedule pressure; allow player freedom and alternative routes to important clues where possible.

## Core mechanics
### A. Dual meaning of ordinary actions
An action should first make sense as everyday life; its ritual meaning may emerge only in retrospect. Avoid displaying every action as ritual points.

### B. Three layers of knowledge
- What the player knows;
- What Mite believes;
- What actually happened in the world.
These layers may differ, but the rules and evidence must remain coherent.

### C. Autonomous villagers
Each important villager should have at least a schedule, relationships, memories/beliefs, and a stress-response tendency. Start with three distinct villagers rather than simulating everyone.

### D. Major transformation
A clear narrative event triggers the change, rather than a hidden meter crossing a threshold. Prior actions shape omens, visible clues, and local consequences; afterward, old content gains new meanings.

## First vertical slice (recommended)
- A small explorable village;
- Several distinct activities for Mite;
- Three villagers with autonomous schedules;
- One forest area;
- A clue journal;
- One major transformation event;
- At least three visible changes to existing content after the transformation.

## Validation questions
1. Does the player choose activities rather than only click through dialogue?
2. Does the player want to compare earlier clues?
3. After transformation, are familiar people and places worth revisiting?
4. Can the main story still be understood after missing one daily action?
5. Do characters seem motivated, rather than controlled only by plot switches?
